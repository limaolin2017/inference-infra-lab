## Concepts discussed

- EAGLE: drafting uses target-model features.
- Model weights versus run-time hidden states.

- Why a legacy prefix-cache hit can drop its trailing block.
- Why a CPU KV connector can introduce load latency.

## Understanding evidence

- Explained independently: **weights** are learned parameters; **hidden states** are computed for the current input.
- **Verified conceptually today:** with block size 4, the last token H of a cached block EFGH can have draft KV dependent on the next token X or Y. The learner explained that X != Y can make the old draft KV unsafe even though EFGH matches. The target model's KV for that matching prefix is still valid.
- **Lookahead Hash explained with guidance:** the learner understood that `block_size=4` uses the fifth token as lookahead and that `Hash(ABCDE)` is one combined hash over tokens/context, not a direct comparison of E's draft KV. This confusion was corrected in discussion; next-day no-hints verification is pending.
